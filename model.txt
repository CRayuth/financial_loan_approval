So far, the sources have introduced several types of intelligent agents, various machine learning paradigms, and specific supervised learning algorithms.
1. Types of Intelligent Agents
The sources classify agents based on their internal architecture and decision-making capabilities
:
Simple Reflex Agents: Act based only on current percepts using predefined "if-then" rules
.
Model-Based Reflex Agents: Maintain an internal state to track parts of the environment they cannot currently see
.
Goal-Based Agents: Use planning and reasoning to find action sequences that reach specific targets
.
Utility-Based Agents: Weigh trade-offs between competing objectives (like safety vs. speed) to maximize a "utility function"
.
Learning Agents: Improve their performance over time by adapting based on feedback and experience
.
2. General AI and Machine Learning Models
AI is categorized by its breadth of ability and the methods used to achieve intelligence:
Breadth of Ability:
Narrow AI (ANI): Designed for specific tasks; this is the only type currently in existence
.
General AI (AGI) & Super AI (ASI): Theoretical models that would match or surpass human intelligence
.
Development Approaches:
Rule-Based AI: Uses explicit instructions coded by humans
.
Learning-Based AI (Machine Learning): Identifies patterns in data to make decisions
.
Learning Paradigms:
Supervised Learning: Learning from labeled data to map inputs to outputs
.
Unsupervised Learning: Finding hidden structures in unlabeled data (e.g., Clustering)
.
Reinforcement Learning: Learning through trial and error using rewards and penalties
.
Generative AI Models: Deep learning models, such as ChatGPT, capable of creating original content like text or images
.
Agentic AI: Autonomous agents that can plan, use tools, and adapt to achieve goals independently
.
3. Specific Supervised Learning Algorithms
The sources provide detailed technical breakdowns for three primary supervised algorithms:
K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN): A classification algorithm that labels an observation based on the most common class among its k closest neighbors in the training data
.
Linear Regression: Used to predict continuous numeric values (like house prices) by modeling relationships as a straight line (Simple) or a hyperplane (Multiple)
.
Logistic Regression: Despite its name, this is a classification algorithm. It uses a Sigmoid function to transform linear scores into probabilities between 0 and 1 for binary decisions, or a Softmax function for multi-class classification
.
4. Optimization and Evaluation Frameworks
To make these algorithms work effectively, the sources also discuss:
Gradient Descent: An optimization algorithm used to minimize the "Mistake Score" (Cost Function) by iteratively adjusting model parameters
.
Evaluation Metrics: Models are assessed using metrics such as Accuracy, Precision, Recall, and F1 Score for classification, or MAE and MSE for regression
.
Feature Selection Methods: Techniques to identify the most useful data inputs, including Filter methods (like Pearson Correlation), Wrapper methods, and Embedded methods
.
